The administration undertook to embark on a comprehensive review of land use in the New Territories.<br>The review of the Small House Policy was underway. | Background Notes on ALS |

| 2004 | January 2004 | Resumption of ALS; Further Measures to consolidate the housing policy. | Chief Executive mentions that the economy is beginning to recover; measures to stabilise the property market are gradually producing results.<br>LegCo panel identified the drop in revenue from land sales and related sources as one factor contributing to the fiscal deficit. | ChiefExec-2004<br>LegCo- PoD-2004 |
| 2005 | March 2005     | A number of improvements were made to the ALS                                | LegCo-PoD-2005 noted that Government land disposal was in terms of the ALS and private land grants, indicating that ALS had become a central compoenent of the Government's land disposal arrangement following the 2004 resumption.<br>LegCo members noted that the Administration took into account their opinions and included more sites of smaller size in the 05-06 Application List to facilitate participation by small developers.<br>Chief Executive noted that property values have rebounded significantly and deflation finally ended in July 2004 after 68 months. | LegCo-PoD-2005<br>Background Notes on ALS<br>ChiefExec-2005 |

| 2008 | n.d. | Introduced some special measures to ALS | Place 10 'hotel only' sites in the 2008-2009 Application List<br>Revive planning and engineering studies on NDAs: small-scale new towns to provide housing land and meet other land use requirements in the future | LegCo-PoD-2008<br> ChiefExec-2008 |
| 2009 | n.d.| Chief Executive reaffirms the Application List System <br>ALS linked to the Government Housing Policy Repositioning | Repositioned housing poiicy (likely in 2003) and the market-driven approach to land supply (ALS), Chief Executive noted that confidence in the housing market has been restored. | ChiefExec=2009 <br>LegCo-PoD-2009 |
| 2010 | n.d. | No policy shift<br>Government indicated they were monitoring the housing market in preparation for shifts in Land Supply arrangements | Concerns arise about the possibility of a property bubble as there was a relatively small number of residential flats completed and they were sold at record prices| ChiefExec-2010<br>LegCo-PoD-2010 |
| 2011 | n.d. | Government re-initiates a government led land sale arrangement while ALS still remains the main axle of land supply | New Trigger: If there is an upsurge in residential flat prices and developers do not actively apply for land under the ALS system, the Government will on its own initiative put up for auction land.<br>LegCo Expresses concern about an over-reliance on the ALS system and calls for alternative sources of land supply.<br> Government to report actual land supply through ALS and other sources.<br> The finance secretary will chair a 'Steering committe on Housing Land Supply' | LegCo-PoD-2011<br>CheifExec-2011 |
| 2012 | n.d.| Government reaffirms the hybrid approach to land supply introduced in 2011<br>Introduction of a Land Reserve where even when land demand declines, land development will continue but be kept in the land reserve and released when appropriate | Government doubles the target number of flats to be supplied per year from 20000 to 40000 | LegCo-PoD-2012<br>ChiefExec-2012 | 
| 2013 | February 2013 | Abolishment of ALS in favour of Government led land auction<br>Government embraces land reserve strategy | LegCo openly question whether ALS should continue | LegCo-PoD-2013<br> ChiefExec-2013|
